# Brief: take this street court to the next level

## What I'm building

A 3D street basketball court that runs in the browser, where brands and startups pay to show up: a fan in the bleachers wearing their colors, a banner on the fence, a mural on the wall, an ad on the big screen, or the court's name itself. I'll market it on X (Twitter) with short videos and screenshots, so the visuals are the product. It has to look good enough that people share it and founders want their brand in it.

## Why it works this way

This comes from a wave of "buy a spot on the internet" sites in Aug–Sep 2026 (outbid.lol, claimavenue.com, cubicles.lol and hundreds of copies). What I took from them:

- **One-time purchases plus public takeovers sell far better than monthly rent.** A takeover means anyone can take a spot by paying 1.25× what it last sold for. Cubicles charged monthly rent and got very few buyers. Claim Avenue sold takeovers and made real money.
- **Most copies made almost nothing.** The winners had a real hook and spectacle. Looking better than everything else out there is how this one stands out.
- **Buyers are mostly founders and startups on X.** They're buying visibility, status and a fun moment to post about, so every purchase should give them something worth sharing.

## Where things stand (scan the repo and verify for yourself)

- **Stack:** Vite + TypeScript + three.js. `README.md` has the commands. The dev server runs on port 5190.
- **3D models:** generated by Python scripts that drive Blender 5.2 headless (`blender/`), exported to `public/models/*.glb`. Blender 5.2 and Unreal 5.8 are installed on this Mac.
- **Scene today:**
  - An outdoor court at golden hour.
  - Bleachers with about 880 seats and an instanced crowd.
  - Fences and barriers carrying banners, and a graffiti handball wall carrying murals.
  - Brick buildings with a big screen and a court-name sign.
  - Hoops, lights and trees.
  - Ten big-head cartoon players running a scripted game with animations.
- **Interface:** an overlay for browsing and buying spots (checkout is a stub), a leaderboard, and demo/empty data modes (`?demo=1` / `?demo=0`).
- **Trailer mode:** press T to hide the UI and fly the camera, for recording marketing clips.
- **Brand spots are data-driven.** `src/data/` holds the state, and `src/data/api.ts` is the single place it comes from. Everything a brand can buy gets repainted from that state at runtime.
- **Screenshots** of the current look are in `screenshots/`.
- **Weak points:**
  - The characters are generated by code: simple faces, blobby bodies, limited animation.
  - The crowd is static poses.
  - Textures are simple procedural ones.
  - Overall it reads as "clean mobile game" rather than the rich, stylized look I'm after.

## The look I want

I'll attach two reference images. They show:

1. A painterly, stylized streetball scene: a big bruiser player in a mesh jersey crouched with the ball on a worn purple court, warm late-afternoon light, graffiti murals, and spectators along the sideline and up on a balcony. Chunky exaggerated proportions and subtle ink outlines.
2. An outdoor park like Rucker Park: a big-head caricature player dunking, a green court, packed bleachers, a chain-link fence. Bright, saturated and clean.

Aim for that tier: stylized console or premium-mobile quality with expressive characters, a lively crowd, rich materials, atmosphere and cinematic light. You're free to change the approach: remodel or replace the characters, rework materials and lighting, restructure the scene, change the rendering setup. Use whatever gets the best result.

## Hard requirements (the only real constraints)

1. **It must open from a link on a phone.** Most traffic will come from X on mobile. That means a fast first view and a reasonable total download, smooth on recent phones, and a graceful fallback on weak devices. A native app or streamed rendering doesn't fit.
2. **Every sellable spot stays data-driven** and can be repainted at runtime (brand name, colors, tagline, link, and later uploaded logos).
3. **No real brands, team names, logos or real people's likenesses** in anything shipped. No NBA teams or players. Demo brands must be fictional and clearly labeled as demo, and made-up sales numbers must never be shown as real.
4. **Third-party assets only if their license allows commercial use,** and keep a record of where each one came from.
5. **Keep a trailer/cinematic mode.** It's how the marketing videos get made.
6. **Don't connect real payments.** Checkout stays a stub until I set up Stripe myself.

## What to work on (my priorities; use your judgment)

1. **Visual quality.** This is the biggest lever. Characters first, then animation (dribble moves, layups, dunks, celebrations, crowd reactions), environment detail, lighting and atmosphere, and camera work.
2. **Make the spots look worth buying.** Murals should look spray-painted, banners like real vinyl, the big screen like a real LED screen, and a court renaming should feel like an event.
3. **Shareable moments.** For example, a short celebration when a spot changes hands, and a share image or clip for each spot a buyer can post.
4. **Load time, performance and the mobile experience.**
5. **Anything else** that makes it more impressive or more likely to sell.

## How to work

- **Start by exploring the repo and running it.** It isn't a git repo yet, so initialize one and commit the current state as a baseline before changing anything. Commit in small steps after that.
- **Iterate visually.** Screenshot the default view, a close-up of the players, the crowd and trailer mode, compare against the references, and keep going.
- **Keep `npm run build` passing.**
- **When you're done,** summarize what you changed, the key decisions, and what's still left.
